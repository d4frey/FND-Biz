1. Follow these five steps

You do not need every certificate at the start. First decide exactly what you will sell and where it will go.

1

Define the product

Write down the type, whole or ground, packaging, supplier and intended use. For cocoa shells, decide whether they are for food, animal feed or another use.

2

Get the business ready

Check the business registration, tax number (NPWP), business ID (NIB) and customs access. Add any permits needed for the actual business activity.

3

Confirm the destination’s rules

Ask the buyer/importer to confirm that the exact Indonesian product can enter, which certificates are needed and who will arrange them.

4

Prepare the product and paperwork

Complete the required tests, certificates, labels and shipping documents. Keep the supplier and shipment records consistent.

5

Check everything before shipping

DAFFA checks permits and certificates, NISYA checks commercial documents, and the forwarder/importer checks customs and arrival requirements.

2. Prepare this basic document pack

What is not automatically required for every export?

There is no single certificate pack for all six products. Do not assume every shipment needs a special export permit, BPOM product registration, SNI certification, a halal certificate or a private quality certificate. Check the actual product and rules first. Halal obligations in Indonesia and the destination must be assessed separately.

Certificates such as ISO 22000 or BRCGS may be requested by buyers. They do not replace government permits or shipment certificates.

Where do the demand numbers come from?

Cocoa shells, clove and pepper use the workbook’s Raw Import tab. Cardamom and nutmeg were combined with mace in the workbook, and cinnamon had no separate series, so the study used product-specific World Bank WITS / UN Comtrade data.

Cardamom’s UAE position is provisional. The direct UAE import figure was unavailable in the WITS table. A secondary source estimates about $222 million, supported by large supplier exports to UAE. It needs a final data check.

Cinnamon combines whole and ground imports and does not isolate demand for Indonesian cinnamon. Indonesia is excluded as a foreign destination; overlapping China records and the EU country-group total are not counted as separate countries. Import demand may include processing and re-export.

Figures are rounded to make them easy to read. Detailed values, source ranges and data differences are in the research reference.

4. What should DAFFA do next?

First

Make one product sheet

Get the supplier, exact product type, whole/ground form, intended use and processing/storage locations from FABIAN.

Then

Check 1–2 pilot routes

Use the market cards to confirm admission, required documents and importer responsibilities for a small starting shortlist.

Before committing

Get costs and lead times

Confirm the certificate issuer, laboratory, label requirements and processing time. Share the costs with NISYA for pricing.

Ready to ship? Only when the importer confirms entry is allowed, every required approval is available, the lot meets the rules, and all documents match.
